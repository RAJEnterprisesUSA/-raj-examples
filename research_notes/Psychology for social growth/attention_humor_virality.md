# Psychology of Attention, Retention, Humor and Sharing in Short-Form Social Video (applied to a small Las Vegas party rental business)

Scope note: research run Oct 26, 2026, about 20 searches and fetches. Evidence-strength labels used throughout:
**[PEER]** peer-reviewed experiment or field study · **[META]** meta-analysis · **[PLATFORM]** platform-published statement or doc · **[INDUSTRY]** industry survey or vendor dataset · **[PRACTITIONER]** opinion or single-creator test · **[FOUNDATIONAL]** older classic research (pre-2015), still widely cited.
Formats the applications refer to: (a) ~17s TREND REMIX comedy video, (b) GUESS THE THEME clue (Wed) and answer (Thu), (c) PRICE IT "how much would you pay for this setup?" image post.
Business rules respected in every application idea: no engagement bait ("comment YES", "tag a friend", "send this to"), no fake scarcity or invented claims, no phone, links or "free" in FB/IG captions.

## 1. Hooks and the first 1-3 seconds (pattern interrupt, curiosity gap, novelty, hook timing, retention curves)

### Takeaway
Platform docs and the one rigorous drop-off study agree that most viewers decide in the opening seconds and most leave early. Curiosity is a measurable reward-anticipation state that makes people "pay" (in time) to close an information gap, and it also improves memory of the answer. The opening frame should show a surprising or incongruous image and pose a question the video will answer, without opening on branding.

### Cited Findings
- **[PLATFORM]** TikTok's official ads guidance says: "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds to boost engagement and increase watch time. You can do so by creating suspense, surprise, or other emotions," and "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness." The same page recommends text overlays and transitions to hold view time. — [TikTok for Business, Creative best practices for performance ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- **[PLATFORM, secondhand]** YouTube says the Shorts feed differs from long-form because "people are swiping through content not knowing what comes next," so the system looks at whether viewers choose to watch or swipe away. — [TechCrunch, Aug 2023, reporting YouTube's creator video](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)
- **[PLATFORM metric, INDUSTRY interpretation]** YouTube Shorts analytics shows a "Viewed vs. Swiped away" (now labeled "Stayed to watch") metric. It measures the opening, not completion. A Short can have a weak stay rate and a strong average percentage viewed. A third-party guide suggests aiming for 75-80% stayed; that is NOT an official YouTube benchmark. — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate); [Buffer, Creator's Guide to Shorts Analytics](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics)
- **[PEER]** A 2024 ACM conference paper (Salminen and colleagues) analyzing TikTok ad viewing found most viewers leave the ads within the first quarter of the video. — [QCRI summary, Engagement Patterns in TikTok: An Analysis of Short Video Ads](https://acua.qcri.org/blog/engagement-patterns-in-tiktok-an-analysis-of-short-video-ads/); [JYX record](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/98478?show=full)
- **[FOUNDATIONAL theory]** Loewenstein's (1994) information-gap theory: curiosity arises from a gap between what one knows and what one wants to know; a small "priming dose" of information raises the appetite for more. — [PMC review of curiosity research (PMC10243256)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10243256); [Kang et al. 2009 full text, CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf)
- **[PEER, FOUNDATIONAL]** Kang et al. (2009, *Psychological Science* 20, 963-973) fMRI study: curiosity about trivia answers tracked activity in reward-anticipation (caudate) regions; more curious subjects spent more scarce resources (tokens or waiting time) to learn answers; curiosity was highest at moderate confidence; high-curiosity answers were better remembered, including after a delay. — [Kang et al. 2009 PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf); [ASU record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-wick-in-the-candle-of-learning-epistemic-curiosity-activates-/); [PMC10243256](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10243256)
- **[INDUSTRY, unverified]** The widely repeated "63% of the highest-CTR TikTok videos hook in the first 3 seconds" stat is attributed to TikTok but could not be traced to a primary TikTok document; sources word it differently ("top-CTR videos" vs "successful ads"). — [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/importance-of-tiktok-ad-hooks-first-3-seconds/); [HeyOrca](https://www.heyorca.com/blog/best-tiktok-hooks)
- **[INDUSTRY, vendor]** OpusClip analysis of 34,635 TikToks (as reported by Segwise) claims ~90% of underperforming videos fail in the first 3 seconds, and that showing the result/finished product up front performed well in their 2026 sample. Vendor data, methodology not public. — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/anatomy-top-performing-video-ad-frame-by-frame)

### Inferences
- (a) TREND REMIX: frame one should already be the incongruity (e.g., a folding table doing the trend's move), with a 3-6 word on-screen question ("Why is the table doing that?"). This uses TikTok's "suspense/surprise in the first seconds" guidance and the information-gap mechanism. Do not open on the logo or end card; brand recall can come from the brand being the punchline (see Q2/Q3). This matches the repo's existing "first frame = punchline / hook text at second zero" rule.
- (a) Kang's moderate-confidence finding suggests hooks work best when the viewer half-recognizes the trend (it is live this week) but cannot predict how it becomes a party-rental joke. That supports the "current trends only" rule: a familiar format plus an unknown payoff.
- (b) GUESS THE THEME: the clue post is a pure information-gap device. Give enough clues for moderate confidence (two or three solid clues, one misdirect) instead of too few (no curiosity) or too many (no gap).
- (c) PRICE IT: the image itself is the hook; the question "how much would you pay?" creates the gap, and the answer is the actual price-list math (only real prices: chairs $2, 6 ft rectangle $10, 6 ft round $15, table + 6 chairs $22; venue $625/$800).
- Measurement: on YouTube, watch "Stayed to watch" as the hook metric and average percentage viewed as the body metric, separately.

### Gaps
- No primary TikTok document was found confirming the "63% in 3 seconds" stat; treat it as directional.
- No peer-reviewed study was found that isolates "pattern interrupt" openings in short-form video specifically; the support is the curiosity/surprise literature plus platform guidance.
- No public, platform-published retention-curve benchmarks for Reels or Shorts by length were found.

## 2. Humor psychology (benign violation, incongruity-resolution, self-deprecation; humor and sharing, liking, purchase intent; backfire)

### Takeaway
Humor works when something seems "wrong" but safe (a benign violation). Humor in ads reliably improves attention and attitude toward the ad, but brand gains are less certain. It backfires when it threatens a specific group, is too threatening or fails to be funny. Humor that is timely and tied to an outside event (improvised, real-time posts) has peer-reviewed evidence of raising virality.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER, FOUNDATIONAL]** McGraw and Warren (2010, *Psychological Science*), "Benign Violations": humor arises when a situation is a violation of how things ought to be AND is simultaneously benign. Three routes to benign: an alternative norm makes it acceptable, weak commitment to the violated norm, or psychological distance. Five experiments. — [McGraw & Warren 2010 PDF](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/mcgraw.warren.2010.pdf); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/08/100809142042.htm)
- **[PEER, replication concern]** A University of Cape Town study argues the original study's methodology was flawed and re-tested it; I did not retrieve its results. Treat BVT as influential theory with some open replication questions. — [UCT](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/media/251707)
- **[META, FOUNDATIONAL]** Eisend's meta-analysis of humor in advertising (*Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science* 37(2), 191-203; online 2008, print 2009) is the standard quantitative synthesis. I confirmed the citation but did NOT retrieve its effect sizes for brand attitude or purchase intention; a secondary deck says humor ads are associated with more positive brand attitudes and purchase likelihood (anecdotal source). — [Springer Professional](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/a-meta-analysis-of-humor-in-advertising/11797632); [secondary deck](https://brandsandhumour.substack.com/api/v1/file/3a4222e7-ea26-4b5a-8d58-515dfc66de78.pdf)
- **[PEER]** Warren and McGraw (2016), "When Does Humorous Marketing Hurt Brands?" (*Journal of Marketing Behavior*): humorous ads can hurt brand attitudes by eliciting negative feelings, even when people find them funny. Risk rises when the humor is highly threatening, makes fun of a subset of the population rather than people in general, and motivates avoidance. In one study a mildly and a severely inappropriate ad were rated equally funny, but the severe one produced more negative feelings toward the ad. Warren: "If you try to be funny and fail, it almost always hurts the brand." — [Warren & McGraw 2016 PDF](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/warren.mcgraw.2016a.pdf); [Eller College news](https://eller.arizona.edu/news/2016/12/those-funny-ads-may-make-you-laugh-maybe-not-buy); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/12/161201121259.htm)
- **[PEER]** Borah, Banerjee, Lin, Jain and Eisingerich (2020, *Journal of Marketing* 84(2), 69-91), "Improvised Marketing Interventions in Social Media": brand posts composed and published on the fly, close to an external event, often witty or humorous, boost virality and even stock returns; the effect rests on humor paired with timeliness or unexpectedness. Five studies. The authors advise room to react to trending topics with care about tone. — [AMA press release](https://www.ama.org/press-releases/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-using-improvised-marketing-interventions-to-build-brand/); [accepted manuscript, White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/154774/1/AAM_IMI_Manuscript_final.pdf); [Leeds blog](https://business.leeds.ac.uk/dir-record/research-blog/1694/improvising-marketing-in-real-time-with-social-media)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Sprout Social Index 2025 (4,000+ consumers surveyed): consumers want to relate to brands and "want to be entertained in some capacity." — [Sprout Social Index 2025 PDF](https://brandfolder.sproutsocial.com/VXTUTVL1/at/c5xxww63p5qnmhqrw5qsrb9n/index_2025_what-consumers-want-on-social.pdf); [secondary summary](https://ifutures.discourse.group/t/social-media-in-2025-what-consumers-really-want-from-brands/8609)

### Inferences
- (a) Incongruity plus resolution is the template: the violation is the trend's absurd premise applied to rental gear (chairs on a runway, a table "merging" with another table); the benign part is that nobody gets hurt and the fix is competent (the crew sets up perfectly). The "competent pro" main character in the AI SKIT RULE fits BVT: the situation is the violation, the pro is the reassurance.
- Self-deprecation that targets the brand or its own mascots (Lil Wobbles the cheap chair, the cat, Mr. Big Business losing) is the safest target under Warren & McGraw's 2016 findings, because it threatens nobody outside the brand. Jokes aimed at a specific group (customers, other vendors, a demographic) carry the backfire risk the study measured. This lines up with the repo's existing "humor from the situation, never identity or stereotype" rule.
- Failed humor costs more than no humor (Warren quote). That supports QA before posting: if a remix's joke needs explaining, drop it rather than ship it.
- Borah et al. is the strongest peer-reviewed support for the trend-remix lane itself: timely + humorous + unexpected beats planned content on virality.

### Gaps
- Eisend's effect sizes (attention, attitude toward the ad, brand attitude, purchase intention) were not retrieved; a report writer should not quote numbers for them.
- No peer-reviewed study was found specifically on self-deprecating brand humor in short-form video.
- No study was found that directly measures whether animated (vs live-action) brand comedy shorts change purchase intent.

## 3. Virality and sharing (high-arousal emotion, STEPPS, motives for sharing, sends vs likes)

### Takeaway
High-arousal emotions (awe, amusement-adjacent excitement, anger, anxiety) drive sharing more than low-arousal ones; positive content beats negative overall. Emotional ads get shared more, but only "emotional brand-integral" content (where the brand is central to the emotion) also lifts brand outcomes. On Instagram, sends (DM shares) per reach is one of three top ranking signals and matters more for reaching non-followers.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER, FOUNDATIONAL]** Berger and Milkman (2012, *Journal of Marketing Research*), "What Makes Online Content Viral?": analysis of ~7,000 New York Times articles plus experiments. Positive content is more viral than negative, but arousal matters most: awe, anger and anxiety (high arousal) increase sharing; sadness (low arousal, deactivating) decreases it. The effect holds when controlling for surprise, interest and practical usefulness; experiments show activation drives it causally. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral)
- **[PEER]** Akpinar and Berger (2017, *JMR* 54(2), 318-330), "Valuable Virality": across share data from hundreds of real online ads plus lab experiments, emotional appeals are shared more than informative ones, but informative ads do more for brand evaluation and purchase because the brand is integral. "Emotional brand-integral" ads (the emotion comes from the brand/product itself) boost sharing AND brand outcomes. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Valuable-Virality.pdf); [VU Amsterdam record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/valuable-virality/)
- **[PRACTITIONER/BOOK, FOUNDATIONAL]** Berger's STEPPS framework (*Contagious*, 2013): Social currency (sharing makes the sharer look in-the-know), Triggers (ideas that stay top of mind spread), Emotion, Public (visible behavior gets copied), Practical value (useful info gets passed along), Stories (narratives carry ideas). This is a book synthesis of his research, not a single test. — [Advisor Perspectives review](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2013/03/26/contagious-why-things-catch-on); [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/contagious-jonah-berger)
- **[INDUSTRY, FOUNDATIONAL]** New York Times Customer Insight Group with Latitude Research, "The Psychology of Sharing" (2011; interviews, a one-week panel, survey of 2,500 sharers). Reported motives: bring valuable/entertaining content to others (94% consider usefulness to the recipient), define oneself (68%), grow and nourish relationships (78%), self-fulfillment/feel involved (69%), support causes or brands (84%). Figures come from secondary reports and vary slightly between them. — [Shareable](https://www.shareable.net/new-research-why-people-share-content-online/); [Contently](https://contently.com/strategist/2012/02/24/psychology-of-sharing)
- **[PLATFORM]** Adam Mosseri (Instagram head), Jan 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends"; creators should watch average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." Earlier (2024) he said sends per reach correlate more with overall reach than anything else, in his experience. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-reveals-key-factors-for-boosting-reach-8646916)
- **[PLATFORM]** TikTok's own explainer lists user interactions (videos you like or share, accounts you follow, comments you post) among recommendation inputs; the 2020 version adds that finishing a longer video is a strong signal weighted above weak signals like shared country. — [TikTok Newsroom, How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you?lang=en); [Nov 2023 version](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/how-tik-tok-recommends-video-for-you?lang=en-MY)

### Inferences
- (a) Make every remix "emotional brand-integral" (Akpinar & Berger): the laugh must come from the tables, chairs, linens, tent or delivery, not from a generic gag with a logo at the end. A joke that works with any logo swapped in will be shared but will not sell rentals.
- Social currency: the fastest-shared remix is one that lets the sharer show they know this week's trend AND gets a laugh. That is the argument for the "current trends only" rule.
- Triggers: tie recurring jokes to things Vegas people see every week (folding chairs at every party, the "who is bringing the tables?" group chat). Recurring characters (Lil Wobbles) act as triggers too.
- Relationship-maintenance motive (NYT 78%): content people forward to the friend who always hosts or the cousin planning a quince. The caption may name the situation ("for whoever is hosting Thanksgiving this year"), but must NOT instruct "send this to..." (owner rule + Meta engagement-bait policy, see Q6). Let the content prompt the send.
- Because sends matter most for non-follower reach on Instagram (Mosseri), and the account is small, sends per reach is the key metric to track for growth; likes per reach mainly reflects existing followers.

### Gaps
- Berger and Milkman studied email-shared news articles (2008-era); direct replication on TikTok/Reels data was not found. Amusement as a distinct emotion was not separately reported in the summaries retrieved.
- No platform-published numbers on how much TikTok or YouTube weight shares vs other signals.
- The NYT sharing percentages come from secondary sources; the original report was not retrieved.

## 4. Saves and practical value

### Takeaway
Practical value is one of the most consistent sharing drivers (STEPPS; 94% of sharers consider usefulness). Platform statements emphasize watch time, likes and sends more than saves. Save-worthy content for this business means real, reusable numbers (headcount-to-table math, price list) presented once, clearly.

### Cited Findings
- **[INDUSTRY, FOUNDATIONAL]** 94% of surveyed sharers consider how useful content will be to the recipient (NYT Psychology of Sharing). — [Shareable](https://www.shareable.net/new-research-why-people-share-content-online/)
- **[PRACTITIONER/BOOK]** STEPPS "practical value": useful tips and advice get passed along because people like helping others. — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2013/03/26/contagious-why-things-catch-on)
- **[PEER]** Akpinar and Berger (2017): informative (feature/utility) ads are shared less than emotional ones but drive brand evaluation and purchase more, because the brand is integral. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Valuable-Virality.pdf)
- **[PEER, FOUNDATIONAL]** Berger and Milkman (2012) found practical usefulness is associated with sharing, and arousal effects held after controlling for it. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf)
- **[PLATFORM]** Mosseri's 2025 top-three ranking signals are watch time, likes and sends; saves were not named among the top three. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)

### Inferences
- (c) PRICE IT can carry practical value if the reveal shows the real math (e.g., 48 chairs x $2 + 8 rectangle tables x $10 = a number planners can screenshot). That turns an engagement post into a save-worthy reference.
- Split the roles: comedy remixes chase sends (emotion), the weekly save-worthy carousel and PRICE IT answer chase saves and leads (utility). Akpinar & Berger suggests the utility posts do more per view for bookings even if they spread less.
- Never round up or invent "typical" totals; only compute from the owner's price card.

### Gaps
- No platform-published statement found (2025-2026) on how heavily Instagram, TikTok or Facebook weight saves in ranking.
- No peer-reviewed study found specifically on "saves" as a behavior distinct from sharing.

## 5. Loops, rewatch and completion; Zeigarnik / open loops; two-part content and cliffhangers

### Takeaway
Completion is a confirmed TikTok signal and watch time a top Instagram signal, so short loopable videos that end where they begin raise both. The "unfinished tasks are remembered better" Zeigarnik effect did NOT hold up in a 2025 meta-analysis, but a tendency to resume interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina effect) did. That supports a two-part clue/answer format more than "open loops boost memory." No direct evidence was found on whether "Part 1/Part 2" helps or hurts short-form reach.

### Cited Findings
- **[PLATFORM]** TikTok (2020 explainer): "a strong indicator of interest, such as whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end, would receive greater weight than a weak indicator." — [TikTok Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you?lang=en)
- **[PLATFORM]** Instagram: watch time is one of the three top ranking signals (Mosseri, Jan 2025). — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- **[META]** Ghibellini and Meier (2025, *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*), meta-analysis of the Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina effects: no memory advantage for unfinished tasks (Zeigarnik effect "lacks universal validity"), but a general tendency to resume interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina effect). They suggest the original effect depended on experimenter authority, task demands and involvement. Secondary sources report different study counts (59 publications vs 37 studies); check the paper. — [IDEAS/RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html); [Univ. of Bern PDF](https://www.kog.psy.unibe.ch/e48276/e1785865/s41599-025-05000-w1_ger.pdf)
- **[PEER, FOUNDATIONAL]** Kang et al. (2009): when curious, people spent waiting time and tokens to get answers, and remembered high-curiosity answers better after a delay. This applies to the delayed answer reveal. — [Kang et al. 2009 PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** A HubSpot writer's informal TikTok test compared videos with an immediate cliffhanger (in the first 5 seconds) vs without; single-creator test, results not reported in the excerpt. A vendor blog (Virlo) claims cliffhanger openings "often double or triple" retention, with no cited data. — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/psychology-hacks-for-making-engaging-videos); [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/why-the-first-few-seconds-matter)

### Inferences
- (a) TREND REMIX: edit the last frame to match the first frame (same shot, same position) so a replay looks seamless; at ~17s a loop costs little and may add watch time and completion. The end card should be brief or the loop breaks; consider brand recall coming from the punchline rather than a long end card.
- (b) GUESS THE THEME: each post should satisfy on its own (the clue post is a complete mini-game; the answer post opens with the reveal at second zero and a short recap of the clues for people who missed Wednesday). Rely on the Ovsiankina "resume" tendency and curiosity, not on a claimed memory boost.
- (b) Do not withhold the whole payoff to force a follow: frustration risk is unmeasured. Give one satisfying beat in the clue post (e.g., "two clues solved by the room setup, one still hidden").
- Avoid citing the Zeigarnik effect as fact in owner-facing strategy; cite curiosity research and resumption instead.

### Gaps
- No study (peer-reviewed or platform) was found testing serialized "Part 1/Part 2" short videos against standalone videos, so whether two-part content hurts or helps reach is unknown.
- No platform-published data on rewatch/loop counts as a ranking signal was found (claims that "loops count as extra views" are practitioner lore here).

## 6. Participation psychology (guessing games, price estimation, IKEA effect, questions vs engagement bait)

### Takeaway
People value what they put effort into (IKEA effect, but only when the task is completed), and curiosity makes people invest effort to reach answers. That supports guessing formats that are solvable. Meta explicitly penalizes posts that instruct people to react, comment a specific word, vote via reactions, tag or share; genuine questions that ask for opinions, help or recommendations are not the target.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER, FOUNDATIONAL]** Norton, Mochon and Ariely (2012, *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 22(3), 453-460), "The IKEA Effect": across four studies (IKEA boxes, origami, Lego), people valued what they built themselves more, roughly on par with experts' creations; the effect held for novices and DIYers but ONLY when the task was completed (destroyed or unfinished work removed it). — [Harvard DASH PDF](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/12136084/norton%20mochon%20ariely%20third%20round[1].pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1777100)
- **[PEER, FOUNDATIONAL]** Kang et al. (2009): curiosity peaks at moderate confidence and leads people to spend time/resources to get answers. — [Kang et al. 2009 PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf)
- **[PLATFORM, 2017 announcement, secondhand]** Facebook's engagement-bait policy (Dec 2017) demotes five types: vote baiting (reactions/comments/shares as a poll), react baiting (asking for a specific reaction), share baiting ("share with 10 friends to win"), tag baiting ("tag a friend who..."), comment baiting (Facebook's example: comment "Yes" if you love rock music). Posts asking for help, advice or recommendations (e.g., travel tips, missing child reports) were stated as not affected. Enforcement uses a machine-learning classifier. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/facebook-demoting-engagement-bait/228071); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/facebook-fights-engagement-baiting-spam-in-your-news-feed); [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/2017/12/18/facebook-downrank-engagement-bait-spammers/)
- **[INDUSTRY, secondhand]** A 2025 third-party TikTok compliance checklist lists engagement tricks such as "like-for-like" promises, unoriginal content without creative changes, and undisclosed marketing among things that can make content ineligible for the For You feed. Not TikTok's own text. — [LitCommerce checklist PDF](https://litcommerce.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Checklist-to-avoid-community-violations-on-TikTok.pdf)

### Inferences
- (c) PRICE IT: "How much would you pay for this setup?" is an estimation question asking for an opinion, which is the kind of question Meta exempts, as long as the caption does not prescribe a format ("comment a number", "drop a 💰 if..."). Do NOT add "vote with reactions" or "like if you'd pay more" (vote/react baiting).
- (c) Close the loop the next day or in a reply comment with the real price math. The IKEA effect requires completion: guessers who learn the answer feel their effort paid off; guessers who never learn it get nothing.
- (b) GUESS THE THEME: make the puzzle solvable by a good share of viewers (moderate confidence). Recognize correct guessers by @name in the answer post or a reply, which gives social currency (Q3) without baiting. This mirrors the Chronicles practice of crediting fan ideas.
- Questions that invite comments without bait: open questions about the viewer's own life ("What theme would you pick for a 40th?") are opinion/recommendation asks; instructions about HOW to respond (specific word, emoji, reaction, tag) are bait.

### Gaps
- Meta's current engagement-bait page on transparency.meta.com could not be fetched (DNS blocked in this environment); policy details above come from 2017 coverage, so current enforcement details are unverified.
- TikTok's official Community Guidelines text on engagement bait was not retrieved; only a third-party summary.
- No peer-reviewed study was found on price-guessing prompts specifically as a social media engagement device.

## 7. Trend-jacking / newsjacking (evidence and risks)

### Takeaway
There is peer-reviewed evidence that timely, humorous, real-time brand posts beat planned ones on virality. Consumer opinion is split (some find brand trend participation "cool," about a third "embarrassing"), and timing decays within a day or two. Platforms now reward originality: remixes must add substantial new creative value, not just overlay or repost.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER]** Borah et al. (2020, *Journal of Marketing*): improvised marketing interventions (posts published on the fly near an external event, often humorous) raise virality and firm value; humor plus timeliness or unexpectedness is the driver; authors caution about tone. — [AMA press release](https://www.ama.org/press-releases/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-using-improvised-marketing-interventions-to-build-brand/); [White Rose manuscript](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/154774/1/AAM_IMI_Manuscript_final.pdf)
- **[INDUSTRY, secondhand]** Sprout Social Index 2025 as reported in a secondary write-up: 40% of consumers think it is "cool" when brands participate in viral trends, 33% find it "embarrassing," 27% say timing matters (effectiveness reported to drop after 24-48 hours). Sprout's own framing: consumers want brands to understand cultural moments, not recreate every meme. — [Sprout Social Index 2025 PDF](https://brandfolder.sproutsocial.com/VXTUTVL1/at/c5xxww63p5qnmhqrw5qsrb9n/index_2025_what-consumers-want-on-social.pdf); [secondary summary](https://kendalldickieson.beehiiv.com/p/what-consumers-actually-want-from-brands-on-social-media-2ab8)
- **[PLATFORM, secondhand]** Meta's 2025-2026 Facebook originality update: views and watch time for original Reels about doubled in H2 2025 vs H2 2024 as Meta prioritized original content. Remixes qualify as original when they add something new (new information, analysis, substantial storyline improvements); reaction videos with no substantive commentary, stitch compilations, reuploads, and low-value edits (borders, added captions, speed changes) are deprioritized; repeat unoriginal posters can be non-recommended and demonetized. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-adds-more-measures-to-ensure-original-creators-get-credit/814764/); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/facebook-updates-originality-guidelines-adds-impersonation-detection-tools-for-creators/)
- **[PEER]** Warren and McGraw (2016): humor that targets a specific group or feels too threatening hurts brand attitudes. — [PDF](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/warren.mcgraw.2016a.pdf)

### Inferences
- (a) The trend remix lane is backed by Borah et al., but only when the remix is fast and fits: build and publish within roughly 24-48 hours of the trend peaking, since the secondhand Sprout figure suggests value decays quickly.
- The 33% "embarrassing" figure is the brand-mismatch risk. Mitigation: keep the trend's format recognizable but make the payoff about rental gear (the brand earns its place in the joke), and skip any trend that needs a forced connection.
- Original animation of our own characters fully satisfies Meta's originality criteria; risk only arises if a remix reuses someone else's clip or audio without substantial new storyline.
- News remixes: positive, new, fun only (product launches, records, mergers as "two tables pushed together"); no tragedies, politics or anything that a humor target could read as threatening.

### Gaps
- The 40/33/27 Sprout trend percentages were not confirmed in the primary PDF text.
- No peer-reviewed study was found on trend fatigue or on local small businesses specifically using trend-jacking.

## 8. Mere-exposure effect and posting frequency / consistency

### Takeaway
Repeated exposure increases liking, but in an inverted U: liking rises with early exposures and then declines with over-exposure. For a small account, that argues for consistent recurring characters and formats with fresh variations, not identical reposts. I found no platform-published data giving an optimal posting frequency.

### Cited Findings
- **[META]** Montoya, Horton, Vevea, Citkowicz and Lauber (2017, *Psychological Bulletin* 143(5), 459-498), "A re-examination of the mere exposure effect": 268 curve estimates from 81 articles; liking shows a positive initial slope with a negative quadratic term (inverted U), peaking and then declining with over-exposure. — [Murdoch University record](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-re-examination-of-the-mere-exposure/991005635870307891); [NSF PAR PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10198302)
- **[INDUSTRY]** Sprout Social's 2025 Index reporting was covered as "brands posting less, engaging more as quality trumps quantity." — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/sprout-social-report-shows-brands-posting-less-engaging-more-as-quality-trumps-quantity/)
- **[PLATFORM, secondhand]** Meta's originality update deprioritizes reposts and minor-edit duplicates, which penalizes the "repeat the same post" route to frequency. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-adds-more-measures-to-ensure-original-creators-get-credit/814764/)

### Inferences
- Recurring cast (Mr. Big Business, Lil Wobbles, the cat), recurring weekly slots (PRICE IT Monday, GUESS THE THEME Wed/Thu) and a consistent visual system build familiarity (rising side of the inverted U). Varying the content within the format avoids the over-exposure decline and the originality penalty.
- (b) GUESS THE THEME as a fixed weekly ritual is a "trigger" (STEPPS) plus mere exposure: the same day and time each week trains expectation.
- Posting caps (e.g., 1 TikTok a day while median views are under 100) are consistent with "quality over quantity" industry data, though no study gives a precise optimal number.

### Gaps
- Mere-exposure research is mostly lab stimuli (faces, words, music), not social feeds; transfer to brand posts is an inference.
- No platform-published, current (2025-2026) guidance with a specific recommended posting frequency for Reels, TikTok or Shorts was found in this session.
